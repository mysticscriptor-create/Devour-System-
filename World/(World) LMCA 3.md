# LORD MARKHAM COREBEARER ACADEMY
## Complete Institutional Structure

---

> Markham is not a school.
> It is humanity's frontline production facility — disguised as education.
> Its ideology is simple: strength is the only currency that matters.
> Everything else — kindness, fairness, mercy — is a liability it teaches you to discard.

---

# PART ONE: OVERVIEW

**Full Name:** Lord Markham Corebearer Academy
**Location:** Oakhaven, Kest — Aethelgard Reach
**Classification:** Tier-1 Strategic Academy (ICA Recognized)
**Reputation:** One of the oldest and most prestigious academies in the Aethelgard Reach. Known for producing commanders, elite Corebearers, and high-ranking dungeon teams. Its graduates fill the upper tiers of ICA-licensed operations across three regions.

**Academy Motto:** *"Steel is made in fire. So are we."*

**Enrollment:** Roughly 3,400 active students at any time across all tracks.

**Funding Sources:**
- ICA Academy Grants (primary)
- Eight megacorporation sponsorship contracts
- Five ancient family endowments
- Dungeon resource revenue from academy-controlled pocket realms
- Competition prize pools and broadcast rights
- Private student tuition (Elite Track families pay heavily)

**The Reality Behind the Prestige:**
Markham produces powerful Corebearers. It also produces a very specific kind of person — one who accepts that the strong take and the weak serve. That belief is not taught directly. It is built into every rule, every room assignment, every points calculation, every meal served. By the time a student graduates, most of them don't even notice it anymore.

# PART TWO: ACADEMIC CALENDAR

**The year is divided into three Terms and one extended Field Season.**

| Period | Duration | Focus |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **First Term** | 14 weeks | Theory, baseline assessment, ability calibration |
| **First Break** | 2 weeks | Personal training / family visits |
| **Second Term** | 14 weeks | Applied combat, dungeon rotations begin |
| **Mid-Year Break** | 3 weeks | — |
| **Third Term** | 14 weeks | Advanced operations, competition season, rank assessments |
| **Field Season** | 8 weeks | Real dungeon missions, corporate internships, ICA joint exercises |
| **Final Break** | 3 weeks | Before next enrollment cycle |

**Year Groups are called Cohorts.**
Students are identified as: First Cohort, Second Cohort, and so on up to Sixth Cohort (the final year).

Students who fail to meet minimum growth thresholds at the end of each Cohort year face one of three outcomes: remedial retention (repeat the year), track demotion, or full dismissal from the academy.

# PART THREE: STUDENT STRATIFICATION

This is the most important system in the academy. Every privilege, every room, every opportunity flows from track placement. Track determines future.

---

## TRACK 1 — APEX TRACK
*(Formerly "Elite Track" in internal records)*

**Who qualifies:**
- Corebearers with exceptional growth projections (top 5% of awakening assessments)
- Rare or high-tier personal abilities
- Students from ancient families or major corporate sponsorships
- Students who pass the Ascension Test at enrollment (exceptional physical and ability performance)

**Privileges:**
- Private single rooms in the Apex Residential Wing (the North Tower complex)
- Priority access to A-rank and B-rank academy dungeons
- Personal assigned instructor for ability development
- Dedicated sparring chambers
- Full academy canteen access including high-grade Core Supplement meals
- Fast-tracked Academy Point earnings by design
- Access to the Benefactor Lounge (corporate sponsor meetings, internship pipelines)

**Expectation:**
These students become the academy's legacy. Commanders, first-rank hunters, ICA executives, sponsored celebrities. Markham's reputation lives or dies on what Apex students achieve after graduation.

**Ethan Harrington is Apex Track. So is Max.**

**The Ugly Truth:**
Apex status can be bought. Not officially. But a large enough corporate sponsorship or family endowment "adjusts" the growth projection formula. Everyone knows. No one says it aloud.

---

## TRACK 2 — VANGUARD TRACK
*(Standard awakened with solid combat potential)*

**Who qualifies:**
- Awakened students with average-to-good growth projections
- Useful combat abilities, consistent performance
- Students who passed enrollment assessment but didn't score in the top tier

**Privileges:**
- Shared double or triple rooms in the Vanguard Residential Block (East and West Wings)
- Rotating access to C-rank and D-rank dungeons
- Group instruction (one instructor per class of 15–20)
- Standard canteen access
- Normal Academy Point earnings

**Expectation:**
Solid frontline Corebearers. Team leads, mid-tier dungeon specialists, corporate contractors. The backbone of the actual workforce. Markham doesn't celebrate them, but the world runs on them.

---

## TRACK 3 — SUPPORT TRACK
*(Awakened with non-combat or secondary ability types)*

**Who qualifies:**
- Awakened students with support-type, sensory-type, or low-offense abilities
- Students who awakened late or show inconsistent growth
- Students reassigned downward from Vanguard after poor performance

**Privileges:**
- Shared dormitory rooms (4–6 students per room) in the Support Residential Hall (South Wing)
- Access to E-rank and F-rank dungeons only, with Vanguard access requiring a special point threshold
- Group instruction focused on specialization rather than combat
- Basic canteen access (no supplement meals without purchasing separately with Academy Points)
- Slower point accumulation

**Expectation:**
Barrier users, buff specialists, medic-role Corebearers, logistics support, analysis teams. Valuable but not celebrated. Most students here know exactly where they stand.

---

## TRACK 4 — AUXILIARY TRACK
*(Awakened with weak, narrow, or practically-useless abilities)*

**Who qualifies:**
- Awakened students whose abilities have no viable combat scaling
- Students who repeatedly failed advancement thresholds
- Students assigned here by corporate or family arrangement (dumping inconvenient cases)

**Privileges:**
- Shared dormitory rooms (6–8 students per room) in the Auxiliary Hall (Ground Level, South Building)
- Minimal dungeon access (supervised group visits to F-rank environments only)
- Reassignment-track instruction (training for infrastructure and support roles)
- Minimal canteen access

**Expectation:**
These students are being trained for roles that keep the dungeon economy running without actually competing in it. Dungeon equipment maintenance, low-tier rift monitoring, administrative support. Not glamorous. Not dangerous. Not remembered.

## TRACK 5 — DORMANT PROGRAM
*(Officially called the "Alternative Contribution Path")*

**Who qualifies:**
- Unawakened Coreborn who have not yet developed an active Core by enrollment age
- Students who attempted awakening and failed (Core calcification, stable Dormant status)
- Politically convenient placements (families paying to keep a problematic child enrolled)
- The genuinely Dormant — those with no Core at all

**Where Marc is officially placed.**

**What Dormant students are told:**
"You have value. Your enhanced Dormant physique is an asset. There are important roles that require your stability and endurance."

**What that actually means:**

**Dormant Career Tracks within the Program:**

*Dungeon Porter:*
Carry equipment, retrieve cores from fallen team members, transport salvage, evacuate injured Corebearers. These people go into dungeons without the ability to defend themselves. The mortality rate is the highest of any non-combat academy graduate path. Nobody puts this in the enrollment brochure.

*Core Miner:*
Extract dungeon crystals from active resource veins. Maintain Null-Stone infrastructure networks. Controlled exposure to dungeon energy for extended periods. The "controlled" part is optimistic. Long-term Core Miners develop progressive cellular deterioration from dimensional energy saturation. They're told this is "manageable."

*Rift Monitor:*
Stabilize low-grade rifts before they escalate. First-response containment in urban areas. Cultivate dungeon flora and fauna in Domesticated Dungeons. The stabilization part requires physical contact with rift edges. Without a Core to regulate energy absorption, this is cumulative damage on every shift.

*Urban Warden:*
Suppress monster outbreaks in civilian districts. Crowd control during dungeon overflow events. Law enforcement in lower-income areas near dungeon zones. Armed, armored, respected by the public, quietly disposable to the institution.

**Dormant Accommodations:**
Shared rooms of 8–10 in the Annex Building — a separate structure connected to the main campus by a covered walkway. The Annex was not designed for comfort. It was designed for capacity. The rooms are clean. That's the best thing said about them.

**Social Reality:**
Dormants at Markham exist in a specific social purgatory. The public respects them in the abstract — "brave service, important work." Within the academy walls, they are invisible. Apex and Vanguard students don't interact with them unless there is an obvious power benefit. Instructors don't invest in them unless they're running the Dormant Program officially.

**The Truth:**
They are consumables. The academy produces them, the world uses them, and history forgets them.

# PART FOUR: ACADEMY POINTS SYSTEM

**Academy Points (AP)** are the internal currency of Markham. Everything requires them. Everything.

**What AP buys:**
- Dungeon access slots (every dungeon visit costs AP)
- Higher-tier dungeon access (A-rank costs significantly more than F-rank)
- Meal upgrades (Core Supplement meals that accelerate growth)
- Equipment rental from the Armory
- Ability coaching sessions beyond standard curriculum
- Private study room access
- Medical treatment beyond basic first aid
- Priority scheduling for competitions and assessments

**Ways to earn AP:**
- Monster kill quotas inside dungeons (they had to bring the remains, which academy uses, either sell or craft)
- Mission completion (academy-assigned operations)
- Competition placements
- Research participation (assisting R&D department projects)
- Instructor-assigned bonus tasks
- Benefactor sponsorship (corporate sponsors can grant AP directly — this is technically a grey area, practically standard practice for Apex Track students)
- Selling salvage materials (that are after the kill quotas filled up) to the academy's processing division

**The Design:**
The AP system looks like a merit system. It functions as a stratification machine. Apex students earn AP faster by default because they have access to higher-yield dungeons. Dormant students earn almost no AP from standard paths and have almost nothing to spend it on. The gap between tracks widens every month a student is enrolled.

# PART FIVE: DEPARTMENTS

## DEPARTMENT 1 — COMBAT TRAINING CORPS
*"Power is forged through killing."*

**Role:** The heart of Markham. Responsible for training all awakened students in combat application of their Core abilities. The largest department by staff and the one that drives the academy's reputation.

**Department Head Title:** Chief Combat Director
**Current:** Director Harlan Voss (B-rank, retired frontline hunter)

**Reporting Structure:**
Chief Combat Director
└── Deputy Director (Combat Operations)
└── Deputy Director (Ability Development)
└── Deputy Director (Assessment & Grading)

**Sub-Divisions:**

*Close-Quarters Combat Division*
Training in melee ability application, body-to-body engagement, Core output at contact range. Instructors at minimum C-rank.

*Range & Precision Division*
Projection abilities, energy-discharge types, directional attacks, distance engagement doctrine. Specializes in ability calibration for range and accuracy.

*Ability Integration Cells*
Small specialist groups (6–10 students) organized by ability type. Each cell has a dedicated instructor with a matching or complementary ability. These are the closest Markham gets to personalized instruction for non-Apex students.

*Combat Theory & Kill Doctrine*
Classroom-based. Covers dungeon engagement patterns, monster behavioral models, optimal kill sequencing, energy efficiency during extended fights. Sounds academic. Is very practical and somewhat cold in tone.

*Team Doctrine Division*
Formation combat, coordinated ability use, roles within dungeon teams (frontline, mid, support, rear). Students assigned here learn how to function inside a unit rather than as individuals.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- Kill efficiency per dungeon session
- Core energy absorption rate post-combat
- Time-to-incapacitation against standard monster tiers
- Survival performance in escalating scenarios

**The Brutal Truth:**
A student who survives every run but absorbs energy slowly is graded lower than a student who takes damage but grows fast. The department's ideal graduate is not careful. It is effective.

## DEPARTMENT 2 — DUNGEON OPERATIONS COMMAND
*"The dungeon is not an obstacle. It is the classroom."*

**Role:** Manages all academy-controlled dungeons. Schedules student rotations. Monitors dungeon stability. Handles emergency extractions. Controls the distribution of dungeon access slots (which cost AP). The second most powerful department administratively because it controls access to the resource that drives everything else.

**Department Head Title:** Director of Dungeon Operations
**Current:** Director Callum Wren (C-rank, former dungeon cartographer)

**Reporting Structure:**
Director of Dungeon Operations
└── Head of Dungeon Stability (monitors energy levels, manages breach risk)
└── Head of Scheduling & Allocation (manages access slots, AP pricing)
└── Head of Emergency Response (extraction teams, rescue protocols)
└── Head of Environmental Records (maps, ecosystem data, anomaly tracking)

**Academy-Controlled Dungeons:**
Markham controls six designated pocket-realm dungeons within operational reach of Oakhaven, ranging from F-rank training environments to one A-rank dungeon reserved for Sixth Cohort Apex students and selected Vanguard in their final year. The Yellow Forest is not academy-controlled — it is ICA-registered with academy access rights, which is an important legal distinction.

**The Misclassification Problem:**
Some dungeons in the academy's rotation are intentionally rated one tier lower than their actual danger level. This is done to "encourage growth through unexpected challenge." Officially, this is a pedagogical strategy. Unofficially, it means students occasionally die in dungeons they were told were safe. These incidents are documented as "training casualties" and the families receive a formal letter and a scholarship fund for surviving siblings.

**The Point Allocation System:**
All dungeon access goes through this department. Every student, every visit, every tier. This gives Dungeon Operations enormous quiet power — a department head who likes or dislikes a student can affect their access in ways that are technically within policy.

## DEPARTMENT 3 — KNOWLEDGE & THEORY INSTITUTE
*"Understanding what you fight makes you better at killing it."*

**Role:** The academic backbone of Markham. Covers dungeon mechanics, monster biology, energy theory, history of the Emergence, ICA law, and strategic analysis. Produces analysts, administrators, strategists, and the theorists who design the frameworks everyone else uses.

**Department Head Title:** Chief Theorist
**Current:** Chief Theorist Daphne Aruul (D-rank, former ICA research liaison)

**Reporting Structure:**
Chief Theorist
└── Head of Core Sciences (Core formation, energy mechanics, ability taxonomy)
└── Head of Dungeon Studies (dungeon ecology, classification systems, portal theory)
└── Head of Emergence History (812 years of documented history — the official version)
└── Head of Monster Taxonomy (creature classification, behavioral modeling, threat assessment)
└── Head of Strategic Analysis (geopolitics, ICA law, regional threat mapping)

**What is taught:**
The standard model. Dungeons appeared 650 years ago as a natural consequence of the First Breach. Corebearers are humanity's evolved response to dimensional pressure. The ICA is a neutral regulatory body formed for collective protection. History is divided into clean eras with clean causes. Everything has an explanation.

**What is not taught:**
Any inconsistency in dungeon behavior that doesn't fit the standard model. Core abnormality patterns. The actual failure rate of awakening procedures. The real nature of the "background resonance" that Corebearers sometimes report at C-rank. The fact that 17 bubbles in the dimensional structure have already collapsed. None of this is in the curriculum.

**Research Ethics:**
Officially, the Institute has a formal ethics board that reviews research involving Core experimentation and Dormant subjects. The board meets quarterly. Its recommendations are advisory.

## DEPARTMENT 7 — COMPETITION & ADVANCEMENT OFFICE
*"Rankings are the only truth Markham admits to."*

**Role:** Manages all competitive events, public rankings, advancement assessments, and the annual tournament. One of the most visible parts of the academy's public face. Sponsors watch competition results closely. Rankings affect AP allocation, track placement, and scholarship continuation.

**Department Head Title:** Director of Competition
**Current:** Director Petra Holt (C-rank, former ranking champion)

**Reporting Structure:**
Director of Competition
└── Head of Internal Rankings (quarterly rank updates, inter-cohort standings)
└── Head of Tournament Operations (event logistics, judging, safety enforcement)
└── Head of Public Relations & Media (broadcast rights, sponsor liaison, achievement publicity)
└── Head of Advancement Assessments (end-of-year advancement tests, track promotion/demotion reviews)

**Events Calendar:**

*Quarterly Combat Rankings:*
Every fourteen weeks, students are ranked within their cohort based on combat performance data. Rankings affect AP multipliers for the following quarter. Dropping in rank costs resources. Rising in rank compounds advantages. The system is designed to accelerate separation.

*Team Dungeon Races:*
Competitive team clears — first team to complete a dungeon route and extract wins. Watched by sponsors. Talent scouts attend. These events create careers or end them.

*Kill-Efficiency Trials:*
Solo performance tests. Standardized dungeon environment, standardized monster population, timed. Your Core output data is logged and compared. Efficiency is the only metric. Style, restraint, and creativity are not scored.

*Annual Ascension Tournament:*
The academy's flagship event. Open to all tracks from Second Cohort upward. Bracketed competition, team and individual rounds, public broadcast. The winning team's names go on the Wall of Ascension in the main hall. Benefactors attend in person. Contracts are offered on the floor. It is the closest thing Markham has to a celebration and it is not really a celebration — it is a market.

**The Rigged Parts:**
Apex students receive slightly larger combat arenas in bracket draws. The scoring formula for efficiency trials uses baseline multipliers that favor ability types common in Apex Track. None of this is written down anywhere. All of it is consistent.

# PART SEVEN: FACILITIES

---

## RESIDENTIAL BUILDINGS

**North Tower Complex** *(Apex Track)*
A separate building from the main academy, connected by a covered bridge. Three towers, each eight floors. Private single rooms for all Apex students. Each room has a personal energy absorption device (a small dungeon-crystal resonator that passively supports Core growth during sleep), private bathroom, study space, and window. The corridors are wide. The lighting is good. There is a private lounge on the top floor.

**East & West Residential Wings** *(Vanguard Track)*
Two long residential blocks flanking the main academy building. Shared double and triple rooms. Communal bathrooms per floor. Basic study lounges. Standard lighting. Not unpleasant. Clearly a step below North Tower.

**Support Hall** *(Support Track)*
A four-floor dormitory building south of the main academy. Four to six students per room. Communal bathrooms and one shared study room per floor. The rooms are functional. The maintenance budget is lower than the two buildings above it.

**Auxiliary Hall** *(Auxiliary Track)*
Ground-level housing in the south building. Six to eight students per room. Older furniture. Communal bathrooms shared between two floors. No study lounges — study is done in common areas of the main building during off-hours.

**The Annex** *(Dormant Program)*
A separate smaller building connected to the south building by a covered walkway. Built to maximize occupancy. Eight to ten students per room in bunk arrangements. Clean. Serviceable. The rooms face an interior courtyard rather than the campus grounds. Dormant students do not have a direct view of the main academy building from their rooms. This was not accidental.

---

## ACADEMIC BUILDINGS

**The Main Hall**
The academic and administrative center of the academy. Seven floors. Contains all classrooms, seminar rooms, the Knowledge Institute's library, and department administrative offices. The Wall of Ascension runs along the ground floor corridor — names of Ascension Tournament champions going back 200 years, carved in stone panels. Dormant and Auxiliary names do not appear on the Wall. There is no policy excluding them. There simply has never been a champion from those tracks, and no one has ever questioned why.

**The Theory Institute Building**
Adjacent to the Main Hall. Houses the Knowledge & Theory Institute and the Research & Development Division. Six floors. Floors one through four are classrooms and open research labs. Floor five is restricted-access research space. Floor six is the classified research level — accessible only to authorized senior researchers and department heads. The elevator requires both a keycard and a Core-signature authentication.

**The Medical Wing**
Connected to the Main Hall by a ground-floor corridor. Staffed around the clock. Handles everything from dungeon injuries to ability-strain recovery to long-term Core damage treatment. The quality of treatment you receive correlates strongly with your track. Apex students get private rooms and dedicated attending staff. Dormant students get the shared wards and junior medics.

---

## TRAINING FACILITIES

**The Combat Grounds**
An outdoor complex of training fields east of the main buildings. Individual sparring circles, team combat zones, ability projection ranges, and one enclosed field with adjustable terrain for formation combat training. Used daily by Combat Training Corps. Access requires AP for anything beyond basic open practice sessions.

**The Dungeon Simulation Chambers**
Six enclosed chambers beneath the main academy building. These are artificially maintained low-energy environments that recreate dungeon conditions without an actual portal. Used for combat training at controlled difficulty levels. Limited by energy density — they can approximate F-rank to D-rank environments accurately. Above that, real dungeon access is required. Three of the six chambers are reserved for Apex and Vanguard use only.

**The Null-Stone Practice Range**
An open facility where students train ability use while wearing Null-Stone gear — equipment that suppresses the user's own energy field to simulate the conditions of dimensional travel or covert operations. ## Which Department Were the Instructors Who Found Marc?

When Marc reappeared in the second layer of the Yellow Forest looking human and naked, there were two instructors present.

They were from the **Dungeon Operations Command Department** — specifically from its **Emergency Response division**, deployed to the Yellow Forest as part of the active extraction operation following the dungeon upheaval.

This is the correct department for several reasons:

**Reason 1 — It fits the operation.**
When the Yellow Forest jumped a classification tier and began showing spatial anomalies, the Director ordered an evacuation. Emergency Response is the team that goes into active, unstable dungeons to find and extract students. That's their job. They are equipped for hostile dungeon environments and have authority to operate inside Yellow Forest under the ICA access permit.

**Reason 2 — Their skill set matches the scene.**
Emergency Response instructors are experienced dungeon operators — minimum C-rank, trained to move fast in unfamiliar environments and make quick threat assessments. The one who spotted monster-Marc in the canopy was doing exactly what his job requires: watching for threats from multiple angles, calling it in, and chasing. When the "threat" turned out to be a naked crying Dormant boy, their first instinct was confusion, not arrest. That's an Emergency Response mind — assess, adapt, contain.

**Reason 3 — It creates useful story friction later.**
Emergency Response instructors report directly to the Head of Emergency Response, who reports to the Director of Dungeon Operations. Their incident reports go up the chain inside Dungeon Operations before reaching the Conduct Bureau or the ICA Intelligence Liaison.

This means:

- The report about the monster sighting goes to Dungeon Operations first.
- The report about Marc (the Dormant boy they found) goes separately to the Conduct Bureau and eventually to the Dormant Program Coordinator.
- The two reports are never formally connected — because they were filed by the same instructors but as separate incidents.

Vera Holst, when she eventually gets access to the Yellow Forest incident file, receives the dungeon anomaly data and the monster sighting report. She does not automatically receive the separate "lost Dormant student found in second layer" welfare report, because that went through a different administrative chain.

There is a gap between two pieces of evidence that should connect Marc. That gap exists because of how departments file reports separately.

**The instructor who spotted monster-Marc and remained suspicious** — he filed his monster sighting report through Dungeon Operations Emergency Response. He is a C-rank instructor named Renn Solas. He will appear again. He is the kind of person who does not let things go when they don't make sense.

**The instructor who dismissed it as Marc's imagination** — he filed no separate report, because as far as he was concerned, they found a confused Dormant boy and nothing else.

Renn Solas's report sits in a Dungeon Operations file. It will eventually reach the desk of the Intelligence Liaison. It will eventually reach Holst. But by the time it does, that detail — *humanoid shape, no energy signature, disappeared before contact* — will combine with other pieces of evidence she's been collecting.

The gap between reports is not just a bureaucratic quirk. It is a structural reason Marc survives the first 100 chapters without being identified.
