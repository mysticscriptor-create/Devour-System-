Empty Circle**

Instead of a Broken Core, the Dormant patch shows a **plain circle — no fill, no detail, just an outline.** 

Officially called the "Open Core" symbol. The academy's documentation describes it as representing "unrealized potential." Every student, instructor, and administrator knows it means failure. Nobody says this out loud because the plausible deniability is the point — it lets the institution be cruel without being accountable for it.

The story value: Marc wearing a patch that is literally just an empty circle is more devastating than a broken symbol. Broken implies something happened to it. Empty implies there was never anything there to begin with.


**3. Cohort year marker**

A simple **sleeve band** — one narrow band per completed cohort year, worn on the left forearm. Thin enough to be unobtrusive. Visible enough to matter.

- No bands: First Cohort, unproven
- One band: Completed First Cohort
- Five bands: Sixth Cohort, final year

The story value here is specific: a Fifth Cohort student with two stripes on their patch reads differently than a Fifth Cohort student with four stripes. The bands show time served. The stripes show what they did with it. A First Cohort student with two stripes already is an anomaly worth noticing. A Sixth Cohort student with one stripe is a quiet tragedy.

Marc, as a Dormant student, technically earns bands the same as everyone else — time served is time served. A grey-trim, empty-circle, one-band sleeve is still a grey-trim, empty-circle, one-band sleeve. But by the time he has three or four bands and people start noticing what he's actually doing, the contrast between the grey uniform and what he's becoming is something the story can use.



**5. One addition not in the original — the sponsor badge**

Apex students with active corporate sponsorships wear a small **enamel pin** on their left chest — the sponsor's corporate mark. This is officially optional. In practice, not wearing your sponsor pin when you have one is a social signal that your relationship with your sponsor is complicated, which everyone notices.

The story value: Ethan Harrington's pin is prominent and expensive. A student who had a sponsor pin and no longer wears it is telling a story they haven't explained yet. A student who has never had one and suddenly appears with one is also telling a story.
