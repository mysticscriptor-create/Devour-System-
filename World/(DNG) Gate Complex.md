# DUNGEON GATE COMPLEX
## Comprehensive Institutional Reference — World of the Fractured Age

---

> *A Gate Complex is not a building. It is the last membrane between what humans have conquered and what is still trying to kill them. Every system inside it exists to answer one question: did anything come back that shouldn't have?*

---

## OVERVIEW

A **Gate Complex** is the regulated processing facility built around a stable Dungeon Portal. Every ICA-registered dungeon of C-rank and above is legally required to have one. They serve three simultaneous functions: **security**, **commerce**, and **control**.

For students and new Corebearers, the Gate Complex is often described as an airport. For veterans, it feels more like customs at a border that doesn't fully trust you. For the staff who work there, it is a living monitoring station that never fully sleeps.

The name "Gate Complex" refers to the whole facility — the building, the portal, and the operational staff together. Individual components have specific names. Students call the whole process "Processing." Veterans call it "Customs," usually with a flattened affect. Staff call the intake flow "Intake." None of these are wrong.

---

## PART ONE: THE PORTAL AND ITS BUILDING

### Portal Placement

The Portal is never centered. In every Gate Complex built after the First Consolidation, the Portal is positioned off-center — typically set into the back wall or one side of the main atrium, slightly elevated on a reinforced platform called the **Threshold**.

This is not accidental. Centering the portal makes crowd management impossible. A portal placed to one side creates a natural funnel, directing outgoing traffic away from returning Corebearers and separating the two flows before they can interact.

The Threshold itself is a raised stone-and-reinforced-alloy platform, approximately one meter above the atrium floor. The portal opens at its center. Two descent ramps flank the portal on either side, with a third emergency drop ramp in the rear. The ramps are wide enough to carry a stretcher at speed.

The Portal is the first and last thing anyone sees. Even off-center, it dominates. The light it emits — a slow dimensional pulse that changes color depending on dungeon status — is visible from the atrium entrance. The hum is constant. Staff stop hearing it within their first week. Visitors never do.

### Portal Status Colors

| Color | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Deep blue, slow pulse | Stable. Open for entry and exit. |
| White, steady glow | At or near capacity. Entry closed. Exit permitted. |
| Amber, irregular pulse | Dungeon in reset phase. Both closed. |
| Red, rapid pulse | Anomaly detected. Lockdown protocol active. |
| Grey, no pulse | Portal sealed. Emergency or decommission. |

Staff can read these colors from anywhere in the building. They are visible through the mezzanine glass, through the medical wing's interior windows, and on small mounted display panels throughout the salvage floor.

### The Atrium

The atrium is the spine of the Gate Complex. Everything connects to it. It is a large, high-ceilinged hall — functional rather than decorative, though older complexes sometimes have older stonework or carvings from their construction era.

The floor is durable composite material, stain-resistant, easy to clean. There are subtle drainage channels in the tile near the Threshold ramps. Staff do not explain why. Students who have done enough runs figure it out.

**Permanent features of the atrium:**
- The Security Funnel at the base of the Threshold ramps
- The Split Marker — a floor-level directional guide separating the Injured Flow from the Stable Flow
- A staffed information desk near the entrance (separate from Security)
- Public-facing notice boards displaying current dungeon status, entry wait times, and ICA advisories
- Overhead dimensional scanners — fixed-mount units that passively scan every person who passes through the atrium (visible as ceiling-mounted panels with a faint blue grid glow)

The atrium is always staffed. Day and night. Someone is always watching the portal.

## PART TWO: THE FLOW SYSTEM

Gate Complexes are designed around the principle of **mandatory linear flow**. There are no shortcuts. Every person who exits a portal must pass through every checkpoint before leaving the building. This is ICA law, not facility preference. Bypassing any checkpoint, even accidentally, triggers a secondary review.

The flow divides into two branches at the **Split Marker** and reunites at the **Final Exit Checkpoint**.

---

### ENTRY FLOW (Outgoing Corebearers)

Entry is separate from the exit flow and typically managed through a side passage off the atrium or a dedicated anteroom near the portal platform.

**Step 1 — Entry Registration Desk**
Every Corebearer entering a dungeon must log in. Staff verify identity via Core-signature scan and cross-reference against the ICA registry. The entry log records: name, rank, team composition, declared purpose (combat, resource, training), and estimated duration.

Students at academy-attached complexes have a simplified version of this — their AP scanner handles identity verification automatically.

**Step 2 — Equipment Check (for applicable complexes)**
Higher-classification complexes perform a brief equipment scan before entry — confirming that declared weapons and tools match the loadout log. This is partly safety-related (no experimental or unauthorized gear inside a dungeon), partly tax-related (if you bring items in, you can't later claim you found them there).

**Step 3 — Portal Entry**
Corebearers cross the Threshold and enter the portal. A staff officer is always stationed at the Threshold during active entry periods, with authority to halt entry if any concern arises.

---

### EXIT FLOW (Returning Corebearers)

This is the main event. Every returning Corebearer passes through the following stages without exception.

**Stage 1 — Security Funnel**

At the base of the Threshold ramps, two security officers are always stationed. Their job is not to intimidate. Their job is to be the first human eyes on every person who exits the portal.

They scan for:
- Visible injury (directs person to Medical immediately)
- Behavioral anomaly (disorientation, aggression, dissociation)
- Energy irregularity (passively flagged by the overhead atrium scanners)
- Foreign materials not in a salvage container

The Security Funnel is narrow by design — one or two abreast. Large groups are staggered. No one passes through side-by-side in a crowd. The officers see every face.

At the Funnel, returning Corebearers are issued a **Processing Card** — a physical card stamped with their entry log data. This card travels with them through every subsequent station and is surrendered at the Final Exit Checkpoint.

**Stage 2 — The Split**

At the Split Marker, returning Corebearers separate into two flows:

- **Injured Track** — Anyone with a visible wound, declared injury, or flagged by a scanner. Directed immediately to Medical. Salvage is handled separately by medical staff or held in the Holding Area.
- **Stable Track** — Corebearers who are physically intact proceed directly toward Salvage.

The Split is staffed by a Routing Officer who makes the call on ambiguous cases. "I'm fine" is not a medical assessment. The Routing Officer's decision is final until overruled by a Medical Officer.

---

## PART THREE: MEDICAL PROCESSING

The Medical Wing is never optional. Every injured Corebearer processes through it. Even Stable Track Corebearers may be redirected here after Salvage if the overhead scanners flagged anything during their exit.

### Layout

The Medical Wing is connected to the atrium by a wide corridor — wide enough for two stretchers side by side. It is always brighter than the rest of the building. The smell is specific: a mix of antiseptic and Core-suppression compounds used during wound treatment.

### Three-Lane Triage System

**Lane 1 — Minor Injury**
Cuts, bruises, ability overuse fatigue, minor energy drain. Staffed by junior medical officers. Bandaging, observation, a brief energy level reading. In and out quickly.

**Lane 2 — Moderate Injury**
Fractures, significant energy burns, core strain, concussion-level impacts. Staffed by senior medical officers. Requires more time. Core-signature is checked against the Corebearer's baseline. Patients may be held for observation before Processing Card is cleared.

**Lane 3 — Severe / Critical**
Anything life-threatening. Internal dungeon injuries, deep energy corruption, creature-venom complications, core destabilization. This lane connects directly to the Internal Treatment Wing — a more permanent medical space attached to but separate from the processing flow. Critical cases may be transferred to external hospital facilities via the Complex's emergency vehicle bay.

### Secondary Medical Functions

Medical staff at Gate Complexes do more than treat wounds. They are also the facility's biological monitoring arm:

- **Energy fluctuation tracking** — They log and compare each returning Corebearer's energy signature against their entry baseline. Significant divergence triggers a report to the Overwatch Level.
- **Stress and cognitive screening** — Particularly relevant for Corebearers showing signs of dungeon psychosis or exposure to deep-floor phenomena.
- **Contamination checks** — Some dungeon environments carry dimensional residue, biological spores, or passive energy imprints that attach to individuals. Medical staff have specific detection tools for these.
- **Ability anomaly flags** — If a Corebearer's ability behaves differently post-run than on entry, medical staff are trained to identify and log it. This flag goes to both the Medical file and the Security file.

Medical clearance is required for the Processing Card to be marked complete. Without it, a Corebearer cannot proceed to the Final Exit Checkpoint.

## PART FOUR: SALVAGE PROCESSING

The Salvage Floor is the largest section of a Gate Complex by square footage. It is also the loudest, the most bureaucratic, and for most Corebearers, the most financially consequential part of the entire facility.

### Core Principle

Everything brought out of a dungeon belongs, legally, to the ICA until it is processed, taxed, and released. The Corebearer earned the right to retrieve it. They have not yet earned the right to own it. The Salvage Floor is where ownership is transferred — in exchange for a cut.

### Counter Layout

The Salvage Floor uses a **Category Lane** system. All items must be deposited and logged by category.

**Category 1 — Biological Materials**
Monster tissue, blood samples, organs, venom glands, plant matter, dungeon-grown fungi, creature cores. This lane has the most staff, due to the variability of what arrives and the need for rapid biological containment. Perishable materials are scanned, documented, and moved to cold storage immediately.

**Category 2 — Minerals and Energy Materials**
Ores, crystals, dimensional residue stones, condensed energy fragments, rare alloys. Weighed, graded, and energy-scanned. Grade determines market classification and tax rate.

**Category 3 — Artifacts and Unusual Items**
Anything that doesn't fit the above categories: manufactured items found inside dungeons, items of unknown origin, objects with unusual energy signatures, anything the Corebearer cannot identify. This lane processes slowest. Every item requires an Artifact Assessment Officer — a specialized staff member trained to identify dimensional-era manufacture and flag items that may require confiscation.

**Category 4 — Personal Reacquisition**
Equipment the Corebearer brought in and is reclaiming on exit. Cross-referenced against the entry loadout log. Discrepancies trigger a hold.

### The Assessment Desk

After category logging, all items proceed to the Assessment Desk, where a senior Salvage Officer assigns:

- **Market Grade** (what it's worth)
- **Tax Classification** (what percentage is owed to the ICA or facility operator)
- **Release Status** (cleared for immediate ownership, held for further review, or flagged for confiscation)

For academy students, the AP system absorbs part of this function — points are issued in lieu of or alongside material payouts.

### Processing Times

Simple runs with standard biological and mineral hauls clear Salvage in fifteen to thirty minutes. Complex hauls, artifact-heavy runs, or disputed assessments can hold a Corebearer for hours. The Holding Area exists specifically for these backlogs.

---

## PART FIVE: TAX AND ASSESSMENT CHECKPOINT

Between Salvage and the Final Exit, there is one more counter: the **Tax and Assessment Desk**.

This is where the financial transaction is formally completed. The Corebearer receives their payout record, their tax documentation, and confirmation that all categories are cleared. Any outstanding disputes are registered here.

It functions deliberately like a final bureaucratic wall. Staff at this desk are not security — they are administrative officers. But they have the authority to hold a Corebearer's Processing Card if any prior stage is incomplete. Nothing moves forward without their stamp.

The experience of standing at this desk after a hard dungeon run — tired, possibly injured, waiting for a clerk to finish their paperwork — is considered a standard indignity of the profession. Veterans accept it. New Corebearers find it enraging. Both reactions are documented in ICA training materials as expected.

---

## PART SIX: FINAL EXIT CHECKPOINT

The last gate before freedom.

The Final Exit Checkpoint is a narrow passage — physically narrow, by design — at the far end of the main processing floor. One or two people at a time. A senior officer staffs this desk permanently.

The checkpoint confirms:

- Medical clearance is logged on the Processing Card
- All salvage categories are logged and released
- Tax Assessment is complete
- No active flags from any prior stage
- Core-signature scan on exit matches the entry signature within acceptable variance

The last point is important. The exit Core-signature scan is not a formality. It is compared against both the entry scan and the dungeon's own internal records. If a Corebearer's signature has shifted significantly, the checkpoint officer has authority to direct them back to Medical or to flag them for an extended review.

A small percentage of returnees — randomly selected, rate varies by facility classification — are pulled for **Deep Inspection** before the Final Exit. This involves a second, more thorough scan by a specialist officer and may include a brief interview. Most people hate it. Most people pass it. The ones who don't are grateful it exists, in retrospect.

Walking past this point feels like being released from custody. That feeling is intentional.

## PART SEVEN: STAFF-ONLY AREAS

These sections are not visible from any public-facing area of the Gate Complex. They are concealed behind unmarked doors, accessed via keyed corridors, or located on underground or upper-access floors.

### Control Room (Underground Level or Rear Section)

The operational brain of the Gate Complex. Staff here monitor:

- Portal energy levels in real time
- Dungeon interior conditions (via tethered dimensional sensors placed throughout dungeon floors)
- Incoming biological and energy signatures from the portal feed
- Entry and exit logs across all active sessions
- All overhead atrium scanner feeds

The Control Room staff manage the most critical decision in Gate Complex operations: **Capacity and Reset Monitoring**.

Dungeons are living systems — not in the biological sense, but in the energetic one. They regenerate. Monsters respawn. Resources replenish. But this regeneration requires the dungeon's internal energy to cycle, and excessive clearing depletes that cycle. When the energy level of a dungeon drops below a threshold — different for each dungeon's class and size — the portal is closed to entries.

Humanity does not fully understand why this threshold matters. The consequences of ignoring it are understood: dungeons that are over-cleared begin exhibiting spatial instability, irregular monster behavior, and eventually fracture events that damage the portal anchor itself. The Control Room monitors this threshold obsessively.

**When a dungeon's energy level hits closure threshold:**
- The portal light shifts to white
- An automated alert reaches all active teams inside via dimensional resonance
- Entry is halted
- Teams inside are given a standard extraction window (usually thirty to sixty minutes)
- If teams do not exit within the window, Emergency Response is deployed

The dungeon's reset period is variable. Staff watch. They wait. When the portal light returns to blue, entries resume. Nobody knows exactly what happens inside during the reset. Expeditions sent in during partial resets do not return.

### Staff Observation Corridor

Behind the Medical Wing, a narrow corridor runs the full length of the triage lanes. Staff can observe any triage lane through one-way panels. Used for training, for monitoring unusual cases, and for quiet coordination between senior medical staff without disrupting patient flow.

### Equipment Maintenance Bay

Attached to the Salvage Floor via a rear corridor. Houses the scanning equipment, biological containment units, and energy measurement tools. Technicians work here constantly. The scanning tools are not simple machines — they require calibration against each specific dungeon's energy signature on a regular basis. An improperly calibrated scanner can misgrade an entire haul, costing the facility money and creating legal disputes.

### Emergency Vehicle Bay

A ground-level vehicle dock at the rear of the facility. Used for critical case transfers to external medical facilities. Sealed when not in active use. The dock connects to a dedicated access road, separate from the public approach to the Gate Complex. When the bay doors open, staff who have worked there long enough know not to be in the atrium.

## PART EIGHT: THE OVERWATCH LEVEL

The Overwatch Level is the top floor of every Gate Complex. In smaller facilities, it is a single room. In larger complexes, it is a full floor.

It is the only place in the building where every significant operation is visible simultaneously.

### What Overwatch Observes

- Direct line of sight to the Portal and Threshold
- Full atrium floor visibility (typically through angled floor-to-ceiling glass)
- Mezzanine feeds from Medical and Salvage
- Live Control Room data on wall-mounted panels
- External approach and perimeter cameras

### Who is Stationed Here

**Shift Supervisor** — The highest-ranking operational officer on duty at any given time. All major decisions route through them. If a lockdown is called, they call it.

**Intelligence Officers** — Present in larger complexes or during special operations periods. These are ICA-affiliated personnel, distinct from the Gate Complex's own staff chain. Their function is pattern recognition: tracking which Corebearers are running which dungeons, flagging unusual hauls, monitoring for ICA regulation violations. At academy-attached complexes, an Intelligence Liaison attends periodically.

**Visiting Observers** — In academy-attached facilities, instructors or high-ranking officials may be present on the Overwatch Level to monitor students' runs. They do not interfere with staff operations. They watch.

### The View

The Overwatch Level is one of the few places in a Gate Complex where you are encouraged to look up. The portal is visible from here at a slight downward angle, the Threshold lit and waiting. During high-traffic periods, the atrium below looks like a transit hub — Corebearers flowing in and out, staff moving between counters, scanners sweeping silently overhead. During quiet hours, there are long stretches where the portal pulses in an empty atrium and nothing moves.

The Shift Supervisor who has worked there long enough stops finding it dramatic. It becomes the baseline state of the world: the portal is open, people are going in, people are coming out, and for now, nothing has gone wrong.

## PART NINE: SECONDARY SYSTEMS AND SPECIAL AREAS

### The Holding Area

Located between the Salvage Floor and the Tax Desk, the Holding Area is a waiting space for Corebearers whose processing has been delayed. This happens for many reasons: Salvage is backed up, an artifact requires extended assessment, a Medical flag needs resolution, or a tax dispute is being reviewed.

The Holding Area is furnished with basic seating. There is usually a water dispenser. Nothing else. It is not designed for comfort. It is designed to make waiting tolerable enough that people do not cause problems, and inconvenient enough that they do not want to return to it.

For storytelling purposes, the Holding Area is where conversations happen that wouldn't happen anywhere else. Tired Corebearers waiting together in a neutral, bureaucratic space. No one is performing. Everyone just wants to go home.

### The Confiscation Vault

Accessed only by senior Artifact Assessment Officers and ICA-licensed Confiscation Staff. Located behind the Salvage Floor, no public access, unmarked door.

Items are confiscated for several reasons:

- **Safety classification** — Items that emit energy signatures beyond legal civilian carry limits
- **ICA restriction** — Items matching descriptions on the Restricted Artifact Registry (objects recovered from Foreign Infested Zones, items showing non-human manufacture signatures, objects with active dimensional distortion)
- **Dispute hold** — Items whose ownership is contested, pending ICA resolution
- **Evidence hold** — Items connected to an active investigation

A receipt is always issued. The Corebearer can dispute confiscation through ICA channels. This process takes an average of four to eight weeks. During that time, the item remains in the Vault.

The Vault is climate-controlled, energy-suppressed, and monitored. Smaller facilities have a single secure cabinet. Larger complexes have dedicated rooms. The oldest confiscated items in long-running facilities are objects whose owners died before their disputes were resolved. They sit in the Vault indefinitely. No one quite knows what to do with them.

### The Lockdown System

Every Gate Complex has a full lockdown capability, triggered by the Shift Supervisor from either the Overwatch Level or the Control Room.

**Partial Lockdown** — Entry gates sealed, Atrium secured, all processing continues. Used when portal status goes amber or an unusual return triggers a review. Most people inside don't notice immediately.

**Full Lockdown** — All access points sealed, including exits. No one enters or leaves. Processing halts. All staff go to assigned positions. Corebearers inside the facility are directed to designated holding zones. This is rare. When it happens, everyone notices immediately.

Lockdown is triggered by: energy anomaly in portal feed, illegal item detected by Confiscation sensors, unregistered biological signature in an exiting Corebearer, active security threat, ICA emergency directive.

The full lockdown procedure is practiced by staff quarterly. It takes forty-five seconds from trigger to full seal. The doors are heavy.

### Temporary Equipment Storage

Not present in all facilities — typically found in larger complexes or those near training academies. Short-term lockers for Corebearers who need to store equipment between runs. Rental by the hour or day. Access requires a valid ICA registration. Items left past the rental period are logged, held for thirty days, and then sold.

### The Void Disposal Unit

Present only in complexes attached to B-rank or above dungeons, or those near Foreign Infested Zone borders. A specialized disposal system for biological or energetic material that cannot be safely stored, sold, or transported. The Void Disposal Unit processes these materials into inert form. What "inert form" means for a living energy crystal or a piece of Foreign Zone matter is not something staff explain to Corebearers. The unit is in the basement. It operates on a different power circuit from the rest of the building. There is no window.

## PART TEN: COMPLEX VARIANTS BY CLASSIFICATION

Gate Complexes are not uniform. Their scale, layout, and staff depth correspond to the dungeon they serve.

### Academy-Attached Complex

Smaller, tighter, managed jointly by the Academy and the ICA. The Salvage counters are simplified — most hauls are student training runs, not commercial operations, so the tax and grading systems run through the Academy Point framework. Medical is well-staffed. Emergency Response instructors rotate through regularly. The Overwatch Level is frequently occupied by instructors observing student performance. The atmosphere is institutional rather than commercial.

The Yellow Forest Gate Complex at Markham is an ICA-registered facility, not academy-owned — meaning ICA jurisdiction applies inside it, independent of academy authority. The distinction matters when something unusual happens.

### Commercial Complex (C-rank to A-rank Dungeons)

Full-scale operation. All systems active. The Salvage Floor is the commercial heart of the facility. Tax officers are heavily involved. Corporate representatives from dungeon-owning entities are often present in a management capacity. The Overwatch Level may host corporate observers alongside ICA Intelligence Officers. The Confiscation Vault is active and regularly used. Atmosphere is transactional, efficient, and professionally impersonal.

### Strategic Complex (S-rank and above)

ICA directly administers these. Independent of national governments. The Gate Complex functions almost as a small forward operating base. Staff are military-trained, not just operationally trained. The Medical Wing is a trauma center in full. The Overwatch Level is hardened. Access to these complexes is restricted — Corebearers must have ICA authorization at minimum B-rank to enter. The portal itself is under continuous guard.

## PART ELEVEN: SOCIAL TEXTURE AND DETAIL

These are elements that do not appear in any official ICA procedure document, but that every Corebearer knows.

**The Queue Psychology.** Everyone coming out of a dungeon is tired, often hurt, and holding something they want. The fact that the Gate Complex makes them wait — sometimes for hours — before they can access any of it creates a specific ambient frustration. Staff are trained to be efficient precisely because of this. A slow staff member is a safety risk. A backed-up Salvage counter with a hundred tired Corebearers behind it is where incidents happen.

**The Overhead Scan.** The passive dimensional scanners in the atrium are always running. Most Corebearers know this. Most pretend they don't think about it. The scanners log every Core-signature that passes through. Over time, a returning Corebearer's full signature history lives in the ICA registry. Patterns emerge. Shifts in signature are noted. Nobody tells you when they flag you. You find out later, if at all.

**The Staff Eyes.** Security Funnel officers see every returning face at close range. After enough shifts, they know what "normal" looks like. They know what "something is wrong with this person" looks like before the person can articulate it. The most experienced Funnel officers have pulled people out of line for secondary review who had no visible injuries, no energy flag, nothing observable — just something in the way they came down the ramp. They are almost always right.

**The Holding Room Silence.** There is a particular silence in the Holding Area during a high-casualty run. When a team comes back short — when fewer people exit the portal than entered it — the remaining team members process in a specific way. They are quiet. They sit apart from each other. They do not talk about it until after they are through the Final Exit, and sometimes not even then. The staff know to move those cases through as quickly as regulations allow.

**What the Portal Sounds Like at 3 AM.** Some staff who work overnight shifts describe the portal as louder during low-traffic hours. Not actually louder — the hum is constant, the energy output is stable — but perceptually louder. Without the sound of people moving through, the building settles around it. Old complex buildings creak. The portal doesn't. It is the only thing in the building that sounds exactly the same at every hour.
